\section{Proof of Corollaries \ref{cor:beta opacity} and \ref{cor:opacity_performance}}
Note that the solutions to the fixed-point problem \eqref{eq:W} lies in $a>h$, suggesting that $\beta>\sqrt{\frac{\omega_u}{\omega_s}}$ always holds. The unconditional expected performance is given by (\ref{eq:unconditional pi}), and $\mathrm{E}[\pi]$ is monotonically decreasing in $\beta$. Hence, Corollary \ref{cor:opacity_performance} directly follows from Corollary \ref{cor:beta opacity}.

To show Corollary \ref{cor:beta opacity}, rewrite the fixed-point problem in \eqref{eq:W} as $0=J(z, \omega_{\eta})$ where

\begin{equation}
 J(z, \omega_{\eta}) = (z-1)\left(z + \frac{\omega_{\eta}}{\omega_u}\right)^2 -2\theta z^2,\label{eq:fpp_z}
\end{equation}
with $z\equiv \beta^2 \frac{\omega_{s}}{\omega_u}$. Also, denote $z_k = \beta_k^2 \frac{\omega_{s}}{\omega_u}$ for $k=L,H$.
Since $\frac{\partial J(z_k, \omega_{\eta})}{\partial \omega_{\eta}} >0$ and $\frac{\partial J(z_k, \omega_{\eta})}{\partial \beta} >0$, the implicit function theorem implies $\frac{d\beta_k}{\omega_{\eta}}<0$ for $k=L,H$.
